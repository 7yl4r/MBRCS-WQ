Vertical structure

Cruises profile the water column with Niskin bottles at surface, mid and bottom depths (depth_class). This page looks at how properties change with depth, and then applies that lens to dissolved oxygen — the “example application: oxygen patterns” idea in the outline.

Depth-class distributions

Temperature, salinity and oxygen all differ systematically between the surface and the bottom; the pigments and nutrients are more variable but still show depth structure. The Winkler oxygen violins rest on few samples per depth class (134 surface, 29 mid, 20 bottom) and should be read as indicative only.

Paired surface–bottom differences

By matching surface and bottom bottles from the same cast we can isolate the vertical gradient at each station. We do this for every analyte; the temperature and salinity numbers below are the headline cases.

3,171 casts have both a surface and a bottom temperature measurement.

  • Surface is warmer than bottom in 70% of casts (median bottom − surface = -0.12 °C).
  • Bottom is saltier than surface in 58% of casts (median bottom − surface = +0.01 PSU).

Chlorophyll a, pheophytin and silicate increase towards the bottom most often; temperature decreases (surface is warmer in ~73% of casts). Winkler oxygen has only 13 paired casts, so its histogram is the roughest of the twelve.

Example application: oxygen patterns

Surface and bottom oxygen track each other seasonally (higher in winter, lower in summer), but the bottom is consistently lower — the signal that would motivate closer monitoring for hypoxia in the Keys.

Warning

The CTD oxygen record is intermittent (see Coverage), so these cruise-mean lines have gaps. Winkler titrations (o2_disc) exist for only ~180 samples and are not used in this particular figure; they do appear in the depth-class violins above, where their sparse coverage is again the main caveat.

Cruises profile the water column with Niskin bottles at **surface**, **mid**
and **bottom** depths (`depth_class`). This page looks at how properties change
with depth, and then applies that lens to dissolved oxygen — the "example
application: oxygen patterns" idea in the outline.

```{python}
#| echo: false
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
import sfer_common as sfer

sfer.style()
df = sfer.load()
```

## Depth-class distributions

```{python}
#| echo: false
#| fig-cap:
#|   - "Temperature by depth class."
#|   - "Salinity by depth class."
#|   - "CTD dissolved oxygen by depth class (one impossible reading above 15 mg/L is excluded)."
#|   - "Winkler dissolved oxygen by depth class (sparse coverage)."
#|   - "Ammonium by depth class (log scale)."
#|   - "Phosphate by depth class (log scale)."
#|   - "Nitrate + nitrite by depth class (log scale)."
#|   - "Nitrate by depth class (log scale)."
#|   - "Nitrite by depth class (log scale)."
#|   - "Silicate by depth class (log scale)."
#|   - "Chlorophyll a by depth class (log scale)."
#|   - "Pheophytin by depth class (log scale)."
log_panels = {"nh4", "po4", "no3_no2", "no3", "no2", "si", "chl", "pheo"}
for col in sfer.ANALYTES:
    keep = sfer.plausible(df, col)
    data = [df.loc[(df.depth_class == d) & keep, col].values
            for d in sfer.DEPTH_ORDER]
    fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(8.5, 4))
    sfer.violin_by_position(
        ax, data, positions=[1, 2, 3], labels=sfer.DEPTH_ORDER,
        logy=col in log_panels,
        colors=[sfer.DEPTH_COLORS[d] for d in sfer.DEPTH_ORDER])
    ax.set_title(sfer.label(col))
    ax.set_ylabel(sfer.units(col))
```

and the bottom; the pigments and nutrients are more variable but still show
depth structure. The Winkler oxygen violins rest on few samples per depth class
(134 surface, 29 mid, 20 bottom) and should be read as indicative only.

## Paired surface–bottom differences

By matching surface and bottom bottles from the same cast we can isolate the
vertical gradient at each station. We do this for **every analyte**; the
temperature and salinity numbers below are the headline cases.

```{python}
#| echo: false
from IPython.display import Markdown, display

key = ["cruise_id", "station", "cast"]
paired = (df.pivot_table(index=key, columns="depth_class",
                         values=sfer.ANALYTES, observed=True)
            .dropna(subset=[("temp", "Surface"), ("temp", "Bottom")]))

diffs = {c: (paired[(c, "Bottom")] - paired[(c, "Surface")]).dropna()
         for c in sfer.ANALYTES}
dT, dS = diffs["temp"], diffs["sal"]

display(Markdown(
    f"**{len(paired):,} casts** have both a surface and a bottom temperature "
    f"measurement.\n\n"
    f"- Surface is warmer than bottom in **{100*(dT < 0).mean():.0f}%** of casts "
    f"(median bottom \u2212 surface = {dT.median():+.2f} \u00b0C).\n"
    f"- Bottom is saltier than surface in **{100*(dS > 0).mean():.0f}%** of casts "
    f"(median bottom \u2212 surface = {dS.median():+.2f} PSU)."))
```

```{python}
#| echo: false
#| fig-cap:
#|   - "Bottom − surface temperature across matched casts."
#|   - "Bottom − surface salinity across matched casts."
#|   - "Bottom − surface CTD oxygen across matched casts."
#|   - "Bottom − surface Winkler oxygen across matched casts (13 paired casts only)."
#|   - "Bottom − surface ammonium across matched casts."
#|   - "Bottom − surface phosphate across matched casts."
#|   - "Bottom − surface nitrate + nitrite across matched casts."
#|   - "Bottom − surface nitrate across matched casts."
#|   - "Bottom − surface nitrite across matched casts."
#|   - "Bottom − surface silicate across matched casts."
#|   - "Bottom − surface chlorophyll a across matched casts."
#|   - "Bottom − surface pheophytin across matched casts."
for col in sfer.ANALYTES:
    values = diffs[col]
    # clip the axis to the 1st-99th percentile so a few large differences do not
    # squash the bulk of the distribution
    lo, hi = values.quantile(0.01), values.quantile(0.99)
    nbins = int(min(50, max(8, len(values) // 20)))
    fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(8.5, 4))
    ax.hist(values, bins=np.linspace(lo, hi, nbins + 1), color="#1f78b4")
    ax.axvline(0, color="k", lw=1)
    ax.set_title(f"Bottom \u2212 surface {sfer.label(col)}")
    ax.set_xlabel(f"\u0394 {sfer.units(col)}")
    ax.set_ylabel("casts")
```

Chlorophyll a, pheophytin and silicate increase towards the bottom most often;
temperature decreases (surface is warmer in ~73% of casts). Winkler oxygen has
only 13 paired casts, so its histogram is the roughest of the twelve.

## Example application: oxygen patterns

```{python}
#| echo: false
#| fig-cap: "Mean CTD dissolved oxygen by cruise date, surface vs. bottom."
o2 = df[df["o2_ctd"].between(0, 15)].dropna(subset=["o2_ctd", "datetime"])
by_depth = (o2.groupby(["datetime", "depth_class"], observed=True)["o2_ctd"]
              .mean().reset_index())

fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(8.5, 4))
for d in ["Surface", "Bottom"]:
    sub = by_depth[by_depth["depth_class"] == d].sort_values("datetime")
    ax.plot(sub["datetime"], sub["o2_ctd"], marker="o", ms=3, lw=1,
            color=sfer.DEPTH_COLORS[d], label=d)
ax.set_ylabel("Dissolved oxygen (mg L\u207b\u00b9)")
ax.set_xlabel("Date")
ax.set_title("CTD oxygen, surface vs. bottom")
ax.legend(title="Depth class")
sfer.time_axis(ax, df)
plt.tight_layout()
```

Surface and bottom oxygen track each other seasonally (higher in winter, lower
in summer), but the bottom is consistently lower — the signal that would
motivate closer monitoring for hypoxia in the Keys.
